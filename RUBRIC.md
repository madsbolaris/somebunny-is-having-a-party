## Introduction

This rubric provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating Magic: The Gathering Commander/EDH decks on a **1-10 power level scale**. It is designed for manual evaluation by experienced players who want to assess deck strength for matchmaking, playgroup balance, or personal development.

### Why This Rubric?

Commander's singleton format and social nature make power level assessment challenging. Unlike 60-card formats with tournament data, Commander relies on community consensus about what constitutes "casual," "optimized," or "competitive" play. This rubric:

- **Provides structure** through three sequential evaluation phases
- **Balances objectivity** with necessary subjective judgment
- **Accounts for format specifics** like multiplayer dynamics and singleton constraints
- **Produces actionable ratings** for matchmaking and deck tuning

### Who Should Use This?

- Playgroup organizers establishing deck guidelines
- Store owners running Commander events
- Deck builders wanting honest assessment of their creations
- Players seeking appropriately-matched games

### Evaluation Time

Expect **50-75 minutes** per deck for a thorough evaluation:
- Phase 1: 10-15 minutes
- Phase 2: 30-45 minutes
- Phase 3: 10-15 minutes

## Overview

### Three-Phase Evaluation Structure

The rubric divides evaluation into three sequential phases, each building on the previous:

**Phase 1: Initial Analysis - Strategic Foundation Assessment**
- Understand the deck's strategy, win conditions, and game plan
- Output: Written summary of how the deck works

**Phase 2: Card-Level Evaluation - Component Assessment**
- Assess individual cards across 6 categories
- Output: Quantitative scores (1-10) for each category

**Phase 3: Overall Deck Evaluation - Holistic Power Level Assessment**
- Synthesize findings into a final 1-10 rating
- Output: Final power level rating with comprehensive summary

### Core Evaluation Principles

Based on competitive MTG deck analysis and Commander-specific dynamics:

**Speed**: How quickly the deck executes its strategy (measured by average win turn)

**Synergy**: How well cards work together and amplify each other's strengths

**Consistency**: Ability to execute game plan reliably despite singleton format

**Resilience**: Capacity to withstand disruption and recover from setbacks

**Disruption**: Ability to interact with and stop opponents' strategies

**Threat Density**: Quality and quantity of win conditions

## Phase 1: Initial Analysis - Strategic Foundation Assessment

### Purpose

Understand the deck's fundamental strategy, win conditions, and intended game plan **before** evaluating individual cards. This contextualizes the card-level evaluation in Phase 2.

### Section 1.1: Deck Archetype Identification

#### Determine Strategy Type

Classify the deck's primary archetype:

- **Aggro**: Deploys early threats to win through combat damage quickly
- **Midrange**: Balances threats and answers, wins through value accumulation
- **Control**: Denies opponents' strategies, wins in late game once control is established
- **Combo**: Assembles specific card combinations for instant or near-instant wins
- **Stax**: Prevents opponents from executing their game plans through resource denial
- **Voltron**: Focuses on making a single creature (often the commander) lethal
- **Group Hug**: Accelerates all players' resources
- **Chaos**: Randomizes game state, disrupts normal gameplay
- **Aristocrats**: Sacrifices creatures for value and incremental advantage
- **Tribal**: Synergizes around a creature type
- **Other**: Describe if it doesn't fit standard categories

#### Identify Win Conditions

**Primary Win Condition(s)**: How does this deck primarily intend to win?
- Infinite combo (specify the combo)
- Combat damage (beatdown, go-wide, voltron)
- Alternate win condition (Thassa's Oracle, Laboratory Maniac, Approach of the Second Sun, etc.)
- Value grind (out-resource opponents over time)
- Mill/Deck out opponents
- Other (specify)

**Secondary/Backup Win Conditions**: What are the backup plans if the primary strategy fails?
- Count the distinct win conditions available to the deck
- Assess redundancy (how many cards support each win condition)

#### Assess Commander's Role

**How central is the commander to the strategy?**

- **Critical**: Deck cannot function without commander (e.g., Yuriko, Krenko)
  - Power indicator: More vulnerable, lower resilience

- **Important**: Commander is optimal but deck has backup plans (e.g., Korvold, Muldrotha)
  - Power indicator: Balanced approach

- **Supportive**: Commander provides value but isn't essential (e.g., Thrasios, many 5-color commanders)
  - Power indicator: More resilient

- **Color Identity Only**: Commander chosen for colors, not synergy (e.g., Golos, some partner commanders)
  - Power indicator: Highly resilient but may lack focused strategy

#### Evaluation Questions

1. What is this deck trying to accomplish?
2. How does it plan to win the game?
3. Is the strategy clear and coherent, or scattered across multiple themes?
4. How reliant is the deck on its commander?
5. Are there meaningful backup plans if the primary strategy is disrupted?

#### Power Level Indicators

**Lower Power (1-4)**:
- Vague or unfocused strategy
- Single linear win condition with no backup
- Completely commander-dependent with no alternative
- Strategy requires 4+ specific cards to function

**Mid Power (5-7)**:
- Clear, focused strategy
- 2-3 viable win conditions
- Commander enhances strategy but isn't critical
- Can pivot if primary plan is disrupted

**High Power (8-10)**:
- Multiple efficient win conditions
- Commander is optimal but has functional replacements
- Highly redundant strategy with many paths to victory
- Can win through significant disruption

### Section 1.2: Speed and Consistency Analysis

#### Average Win Turn

**In an uncontested goldfish scenario (no opponent interaction), what turn can this deck typically win?**

Goldfish the deck mentally or physically:
1. Assume average draws (not nut draws, not terrible draws)
2. Play out turns as if opponents don't interact
3. Identify the turn where the deck would secure victory
4. Repeat 3-5 times for consistency check
5. Calculate average win turn

**Win turn categories**:
- Turn 2-3: Explosive fast combo (cEDH territory)
- Turn 4-6: Fast combo or highly aggressive (high power to cEDH)
- Turn 7-9: Focused midrange or combo (optimized casual to high power)
- Turn 10-14: Slower value or casual strategies (casual to focused)
- Turn 15+: Very casual or battlecruiser (precon to casual)

#### Game Stage Capabilities

**Early Game (Turns 1-3)**: What does the deck do in the opening turns?
- Ramp and mana acceleration?
- Card selection (tutors, scry, draw)?
- Early threats or stax pieces?
- Minimal activity (slow start)?

**Mid Game (Turns 4-6)**: How does the deck develop its board?
- Engine building and value generation?
- Deploying threats?
- Interaction and answers?
- Assembling combo pieces?

**Late Game (Turns 7+)**: How does the deck close out games?
- Win condition deployment?
- Recovery from disruption?
- Overwhelming value?
- Struggling to close (no clear closer)?

#### Consistency Assessment

**How consistently can this deck execute its game plan?**

Consider:
- Tutors and card selection (can the deck find its pieces?)
- Redundancy (multiple cards that fill similar roles?)
- Card draw (can the deck dig for answers?)
- Mulligan quality (does the deck need specific opening hands?)

**Consistency indicators**:
- **High consistency**: 6+ tutors, redundant effects, strong card draw, wins on similar turns each game
- **Moderate consistency**: 2-4 tutors, some redundancy, decent card draw, wins within a 3-4 turn window
- **Low consistency**: 0-1 tutors, singleton effects, minimal draw, highly variable win turns

#### Evaluation Questions

1. On average, what turn can this deck win in an uncontested game?
2. How consistent is the deck at executing its game plan?
3. Does the deck have meaningful plays at every stage of the game, or does it have dead turns?
4. Can the deck recover if it stumbles early?

#### Power Level Indicators

- **Power 1-3**: Wins turn 15+, inconsistent setup, many dead turns early game
- **Power 4-5**: Wins turn 10-14, reliable mid-game presence, some early ramp
- **Power 6-7**: Wins turn 7-9, strong early setup, consistent execution
- **Power 8-9**: Wins turn 4-6, explosive early game, highly consistent with tutors
- **Power 10 (cEDH)**: Wins turn 2-4, optimized for maximum speed, extreme consistency

### Section 1.3: Game Plan Documentation

#### Document the Ideal Game

Write a brief narrative of how the deck ideally wants to play:

**Turn-by-Turn Sequencing** (example format):
- Turns 1-2: Play lands, drop fast mana (Sol Ring, signets)
- Turns 3-4: Deploy commander or key engine pieces
- Turns 5-6: Establish value engines, protect board
- Turn 7+: Execute win condition through combo or overwhelming advantage

**Critical Mass Requirements**:
- What pieces need to be assembled for the deck to win?
- How many cards are required for the primary win condition?
- What is the minimum viable board state?

**Pivot Points**:
- When can the deck change strategies if needed?
- What are the decision points (go for combo vs. grind value)?
- How does the deck adapt to different opponents?

#### Phase 1 Output: Strategy Summary

Write a **3-5 sentence summary** that captures:
1. The deck's primary strategy and archetype
2. How it typically wins (primary and backup win conditions)
3. Expected game timeline (average win turn)
4. Commander's role in the strategy
5. Key strengths or weaknesses of the approach

**Example:**
> "This Kinnan, Bonder Prodigy deck is a high-powered combo-control strategy that aims to win between turns 4-6 through infinite mana combos (Basalt Monolith + Rings of Brighthearth) or Thassa's Oracle lines. Kinnan is critical as a mana doubler and card advantage engine, but the deck includes backup card draw and redundant combo pieces. The deck ramps explosively turns 1-3 with artifact mana, establishes Kinnan or other engines turn 3-4, and typically combos off turn 4-6 with protection. Primary weakness is commander dependence—repeated removal of Kinnan significantly slows the deck."

## Phase 2: Card-Level Evaluation - Component Assessment

### Purpose

Assess how well each card contributes to the deck's strategy, and evaluate card quality relative to power level expectations. This phase produces quantitative scores for 6 major categories.

---

### Section 2.1: Mana Base Evaluation (Lands + Ramp)

#### Land Count

Count total lands in the 99:
- 33-34 lands: Very low (requires significant artifact ramp)
- 35-37 lands: Low to moderate (typical for optimized decks)
- 38-40 lands: Moderate to high (typical for casual decks)
- 41+ lands: High (rarely seen, indicates slow/ramp-heavy strategies)

#### Land Quality Assessment

Categorize each land by tier:

**Tier 1 - Premium Lands** (untapped, excellent fixing/utility):
- ABUR Dual Lands (Tropical Island, Underground Sea, etc.)
- Fetch Lands (Scalding Tarn, Misty Rainforest, etc.)
- Shock Lands (Steam Vents, Breeding Pool, etc.)
- Fast Mana Lands (Ancient Tomb, Mana Confluence, City of Brass)
- Utility Lands (Boseiju, Cavern of Souls, Strip Mine, Wasteland)

**Tier 2 - Good Lands** (mostly untapped or strong utility):
- Filter Lands (Cascade Bluffs, Mystic Gate, etc.)
- Pain Lands (Adarkar Wastes, Underground River, etc.)
- Check Lands (Glacial Fortress, Dragonskull Summit, etc.)
- Horizon Lands (Horizon Canopy, Fiery Islet, etc.)
- Fast Lands (Spirebluff Canal, Blooming Marsh, etc.)
- Talismans counted here if land-equivalents are considered

**Tier 3 - Decent Lands** (situationally good or slow):
- Tango Lands/Battle Lands (Prairie Stream, Cinder Glade, etc.)
- Slow Fetch Lands (Terramorphic Expanse, Evolving Wilds)
- Bounce Lands/Karoo Lands (Simic Growth Chamber, Izzet Boilery, etc.)
- Reveal Lands (Port Town, Foreboding Ruins, etc.)
- Pathways (Barkchannel Pathway, etc.)

**Tier 4 - Budget/Weak Lands** (enter tapped, minimal upside):
- Gain Lands/Lifegain Lands (Tranquil Cove, Dismal Backwater, etc.)
- Tri-lands that enter tapped (Arcane Sanctum, Crumbling Necropolis, etc.)
- Gates
- Tap lands with minor upside (Scry lands, etc.)
- Basic Lands (context-dependent—sometimes correct, sometimes filler)

**Calculate land quality percentage**:
- % of lands that are Tier 1-2 (untapped, good fixing)
- Higher % = better mana base

#### Ramp Package Assessment

**Count ramp sources** (8-12 is typical for most decks):

**Fast Mana (0-2 CMC)** - CRITICAL FOR POWER LEVEL:
- **Tier 1 (0 CMC)**: Mana Crypt, Mox Diamond, Chrome Mox, Jeweled Lotus, Lion's Eye Diamond, Lotus Petal
- **Tier 2 (1 CMC)**: Sol Ring, Mana Vault, Dark Ritual, Elvish Mystic, Birds of Paradise, Noble Hierarch, Carpet of Flowers
- **Tier 3 (2 CMC)**: Arcane Signet, Signets (all colors), Talismans (all colors), Fellwar Stone, Nature's Lore, Three Visits, Farseek, Rampant Growth

**Efficient Ramp (3 CMC)**:
- Chromatic Lantern, Coalition Relic, Worn Powerstone, Cultivate-style effects

**Slower Ramp (4+ CMC)**:
- Kodama's Reach, Skyshroud Claim, Solemn Simulacrum, Explosive Vegetation

**Count your fast mana pieces (0-2 CMC)**: This number is a strong power level indicator.
- 0-1 pieces: Low power
- 2-3 pieces: Low-mid power
- 4-5 pieces: Mid-high power
- 6-7 pieces: High power
- 8+ pieces: Competitive power

**Calculate average CMC of ramp package**:
- Average CMC ≤ 2.0: Excellent
- Average CMC 2.1-2.5: Good
- Average CMC 2.6-3.0: Moderate
- Average CMC 3.1+: Slow

#### Color Fixing Assessment

**Can the deck cast its spells on time?**
- Does the mana base support the color requirements?
- Are there sufficient sources for each color (especially for splash colors)?
- Is there color fixing for multicolor spells?

**Rule of thumb**: For each color in your identity, aim for sources equal to 10-12 sources minimum.

#### Section 2.1 Scoring

**Mana Base Quality Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: 30-33 lands, 80%+ Tier 1-2 lands, perfect color fixing, fetch/dual heavy
- **9**: 32-34 lands, 70-80% Tier 1-2 lands, excellent fixing
- **8**: 34-35 lands, 60-70% Tier 1-2 lands, good fixing
- **7**: 35-36 lands, 50-60% Tier 1-2 lands, solid fixing
- **6**: 36-37 lands, 40-50% Tier 1-2 lands, decent fixing
- **5**: 37-38 lands, 30-40% Tier 1-2 lands, basic fixing
- **4**: 38-39 lands, 20-30% Tier 1-2 lands, many tap lands
- **3**: 39-40 lands, 10-20% Tier 1-2 lands, mostly tap lands
- **2**: 40+ lands, <10% Tier 1-2 lands, poor fixing
- **1**: 40+ lands, all basics and tap lands, no fixing

**Fast Mana Density Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: 8+ fast mana pieces
- **9**: 6-7 fast mana pieces
- **8**: 5 fast mana pieces
- **7**: 4 fast mana pieces
- **6**: 3 fast mana pieces
- **5**: 2 fast mana pieces
- **4**: 1 fast mana piece + other inefficient ramp
- **3**: Sol Ring + slower ramp only
- **2**: Sol Ring only
- **1**: No fast mana at all

### Section 2.2: Card Draw and Card Advantage

#### Card Draw Density

**Count all sources of card draw** (8-12 is typical):

**Burst Draw** (draw multiple cards at once):
- Wheel effects: Wheel of Fortune, Windfall, Reforge the Soul, etc.
- Large one-shot draws: Blue Sun's Zenith, Pull from Tomorrow, Skullclamp triggers, Necropotence, Ad Nauseam

**Incremental Draw** (ongoing value engines):
- Enchantments: Rhystic Study, Mystic Remora, Phyrexian Arena, Sylvan Library, Necropotence
- Artifacts: The One Ring, Esper Sentinel, Sensei's Divining Top
- Creatures: Dark Confidant, Ledger Shredder, Consecrated Sphinx, Tymna
- Planeswalkers: Teferi, Hero of Dominaria; Jeska's Will

**Card Selection** (tutors, filtering, scry):
- Tutors: Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Mystical Tutor, Worldly Tutor, Enlightened Tutor, etc.
- Top-deck manipulation: Sensei's Divining Top, Scroll Rack, Brainstorm, Ponder, Preordain
- Impulse draw: Underworld Breach, Jeska's Will, Light Up the Stage

#### Card Advantage Engines

**Repeatable engines** (generate value every turn or trigger):
- Examples: Rhystic Study (every opponent's spell), Mystic Remora, Skullclamp (on creature death)

**Conditional engines** (trigger-based):
- Examples: Beast Whisperer (when you cast creatures), The Great Henge, Welcoming Vampire

**Efficiency** (CMC vs. cards drawn):
- High efficiency: Skullclamp (1 mana, draws 2+ cards per activation), Rhystic Study (3 mana, draws many cards)
- Moderate efficiency: Phyrexian Arena (3 mana, 1 card per turn)
- Low efficiency: Staff of Nin (6 mana, 1 card per turn)

#### Recovery Capability

**Can the deck recover from board wipes or mass discard?**
- Graveyard recursion (Eternal Witness, Regrowth, etc.)
- Draw engines that rebuild hand quickly
- Resilient permanents (indestructible, hexproof)

#### Tutor Count

**Count all tutors** (any card that searches library for specific cards):
- **Unrestricted tutors**: Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Worldly Tutor, Enlightened Tutor, Mystical Tutor, etc.
- **Restricted tutors**: Merchant Scroll (for instants), Gamble, etc.
- **Creature tutors**: Chord of Calling, Eladamri's Call, Finale of Devastation
- **Land tutors**: Not counted here (covered in ramp section)

#### Evaluation Questions

1. How many cards can this deck draw in an average game?
2. Can the deck recover from board wipes or discard?
3. Does the deck run out of gas in long games?
4. How many tutors does the deck have to find specific answers or combo pieces?

#### Section 2.2 Scoring

**Card Draw/Advantage Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: 15+ draw sources, 6+ tutors, wheels, near-perfect card selection
- **9**: 12-14 draw sources, 5-6 tutors, excellent engines
- **8**: 10-12 draw sources, 3-4 tutors, strong engines
- **7**: 9-10 draw sources, 2-3 tutors, good card advantage
- **6**: 7-8 draw sources, 1-2 tutors, decent card advantage
- **5**: 6-7 draw sources, 0-1 tutors, moderate card advantage
- **4**: 4-5 draw sources, no tutors
- **3**: 3 draw sources, very limited
- **2**: 1-2 draw sources
- **1**: 0-1 draw sources, will run out of cards quickly

---

### Section 2.3: Interaction and Removal

#### Interaction Density

**Count all interaction pieces** (8-12 is typical, high power runs 12-15+):

**Spot Removal** (single target):
- Creature removal: Swords to Plowshares, Path to Exile, Fatal Push, Terminate, Beast Within, etc.
- Permanent removal: Generous Gift, Chaos Warp, Abrupt Decay, Assassin's Trophy
- Bounce: Cyclonic Rift, Consign to Memory

**Board Wipes** (mass removal):
- Creature wipes: Wrath of God, Damnation, Toxic Deluge, Blasphemous Act, Cyclonic Rift (overloaded)
- Permanent wipes: Austere Command, Merciless Eviction, Farewell

**Counterspells** (stack interaction):
- Free counters: Force of Will, Force of Negation, Fierce Guardianship, Pact of Negation
- Efficient counters (1-2 CMC): Swan Song, Counterspell, Arcane Denial, Delay, Mana Drain, An Offer You Can't Refuse
- Expensive counters (3+ CMC): Forbid, Desertion, Cryptic Command

**Targeted Permanent Removal**:
- Artifact/Enchantment removal: Nature's Claim, Fragmentize, Naturalize, Wear // Tear, Rec Sage
- Graveyard hate: Bojuka Bog, Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void, Tormod's Crypt

**Stax Pieces** (proactive disruption):
- Resource denial: Winter Orb, Static Orb, Trinisphere, Sphere of Resistance
- Tax effects: Thalia, Guardian of Thraben; Aven Mindcensor; Opposition Agent

#### Interaction Quality

**Efficiency** (CMC vs. effect):
- **Excellent (0-1 CMC)**: Force of Will, Swords to Plowshares, Fatal Push, Mental Misstep
- **Good (2 CMC)**: Counterspell, Assassin's Trophy, Cyclonic Rift
- **Moderate (3 CMC)**: Beast Within, Generous Gift, Toxic Deluge
- **Expensive (4+ CMC)**: Supreme Verdict, Merciless Eviction

**Flexibility** (can it hit multiple targets or card types?):
- Flexible: Beast Within (any permanent), Generous Gift, Chaos Warp, Anguished Unmaking
- Narrow: Terror (only non-black creatures), Naturalize (only artifacts/enchantments)

**Speed** (instant vs. sorcery):
- **Instant speed**: Higher value, can respond to threats
- **Sorcery speed**: Lower value, proactive only

#### Coverage Assessment

**Can the deck answer threats across all permanent types?**
- Creatures: Yes/No (spot removal, board wipes)
- Artifacts: Yes/No
- Enchantments: Yes/No
- Planeswalkers: Yes/No
- Spells on the stack: Yes/No (counterspells)
- Graveyard threats: Yes/No (graveyard hate)
- Non-permanents (activated abilities, triggers): Yes/No (Stifle, Disallow)

**Does the deck have stack interaction?**
- Blue decks: Should have counterspells
- Non-blue decks: Limited options (Red Elemental Blast, Pyroblast, Tibalt's Trickery)

#### Protection

**Can the deck protect its own game plan?**
- Counterspells to protect combos
- Hexproof/Indestructible for key permanents
- Recursion to rebuild after disruption

#### Evaluation Questions

1. Can the deck answer threats across all permanent types?
2. How efficiently can it interact (CMC/mana cost vs. effect)?
3. Does it have instant-speed interaction for surprise removal?
4. Can it protect its own game plan from disruption?
5. Is the interaction proactive (stax) or reactive (removal/counters)?

#### Section 2.3 Scoring

**Interaction Quality Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: 15+ pieces, free spells, perfect efficiency, instant speed prioritized, full coverage
- **9**: 12-14 pieces, mostly efficient (0-2 CMC), mostly instant speed, excellent coverage
- **8**: 10-12 pieces, good efficiency mix, good instant speed representation, good coverage
- **7**: 9-10 pieces, moderate efficiency, some instant speed, solid coverage
- **6**: 7-8 pieces, mix of speeds and efficiency, decent coverage
- **5**: 6-7 pieces, mostly sorcery speed, basic coverage
- **4**: 4-5 pieces, expensive (4+ CMC average), limited coverage
- **3**: 3 pieces, very expensive, narrow coverage
- **2**: 1-2 pieces, minimal interaction
- **1**: 0-1 pieces, essentially no interaction

### Section 2.4: Synergy and Card Quality

#### Role Classification

For each card in the deck (excluding lands), classify its role:

**Engine**: Generates ongoing value or advantage
- Examples: Rhystic Study (draws cards), Smothering Tithe (generates mana), Tymna (draws cards)

**Payoff**: Benefits from synergies, serves as a win condition
- Examples: Aetherflux Reservoir (wins with lifegain), Craterhoof Behemoth (wins with creatures), Thassa's Oracle (wins with empty library)

**Enabler**: Sets up combos or synergies, facilitates the game plan
- Examples: Entomb (enables graveyard strategies), Flash (enables instant-speed combos), Mana dorks (enable early plays)

**Utility**: Provides necessary functions (removal, ramp, draw)
- Examples: Swords to Plowshares (removal), Sol Ring (ramp), Counterspell (protection)

**Filler**: Low synergy with deck theme, minimal impact, "good stuff" cards that don't advance the game plan
- Examples: Random creatures with no synergy, generic spells that don't fit the strategy

#### Synergy Tier Assessment

For each card, rate its synergy with the deck's strategy on a 1-10 scale, then group into tiers:

**Tier 1 - Core (Synergy 9-10/10)**:
- Essential to primary strategy
- High synergy with multiple other cards
- Directly advances or completes the win condition
- Examples in a Zur deck: Necropotence, Phyrexian Unlife, Solemnity, Steel of the Godhead

**Tier 2 - Strong (Synergy 7-8/10)**:
- Significant contribution to strategy
- Good synergy with several cards
- Strong support for win conditions
- Examples in a Zur deck: Diplomatic Immunity, Grasp of Fate, Mystic Remora

**Tier 3 - Support (Synergy 5-6/10)**:
- Helpful but not critical
- Moderate synergy with some cards
- General utility that aids the strategy
- Examples in a Zur deck: Counterspell, Swords to Plowshares, Fact or Fiction

**Tier 4 - Weak (Synergy 3-4/10)**:
- Minimal synergy with deck theme
- "Good stuff" cards that are generically powerful
- Could be in many different decks
- Examples in a Zur deck: Rhystic Study (good card, but not Zur-specific)

**Tier 5 - Filler (Synergy 1-2/10)**:
- No meaningful synergy
- Low impact on game plan
- Budget alternatives or questionable inclusions
- Examples: Random creatures with no synergy, overcosted spells

#### Calculate Synergy Distribution

Count cards in each tier:
- Tier 1 (Core): ___ cards
- Tier 2 (Strong): ___ cards
- Tier 3 (Support): ___ cards
- Tier 4 (Weak): ___ cards
- Tier 5 (Filler): ___ cards

**Calculate Tier 1-2 percentage**: (Tier 1 + Tier 2) / (Total non-land cards) × 100%

This percentage is a key power level indicator:
- 80%+: Extremely focused, high power
- 60-79%: Well-focused, mid-high power
- 40-59%: Moderately focused, mid power
- 20-39%: Unfocused, low-mid power
- <20%: Very unfocused, low power

#### Individual Card Power Assessment

Identify the power level of individual cards:

**Format Staples** (universally powerful in Commander):
- Examples: Sol Ring, Rhystic Study, Cyclonic Rift, Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Mana Crypt, Smothering Tithe

**Archetype Staples** (best-in-class for the strategy):
- Combo decks: Thassa's Oracle, Demonic Consultation, Ad Nauseam
- Stax: Winter Orb, Stasis, Trinisphere
- Graveyard: Entomb, Reanimate, Living Death

**Budget Alternatives** (functional but sub-optimal):
- Examples: Solemn Simulacrum instead of Mana Crypt, Murder instead of Toxic Deluge, Cancel instead of Counterspell

**Bulk/Commons** (low power floor, minimal impact):
- Examples: Colossal Dreadmaw, Divination, Lava Axe

#### Dead Cards

**Identify "dead cards"**: Cards that don't advance the game plan or provide value:
- Cards that are only good in specific situations (narrow combo pieces with no other utility)
- Cards that are actively bad in the current meta
- Cards that are outclassed by available alternatives
- Cards included for budget reasons that significantly hurt the deck

#### Evaluation Questions

1. What percentage of cards are Tier 1-2 (core/strong synergy)?
2. How many format staples does the deck include?
3. Are there obvious budget replacements that significantly hurt power?
4. How many "dead cards" are there (cards that don't contribute)?
5. Is the deck focused on a single strategy, or is it trying to do too many things?

#### Section 2.4 Scoring

**Synergy Density Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: 90%+ Tier 1-2 cards, zero filler, perfect focus
- **9**: 80-89% Tier 1-2 cards, minimal filler, excellent focus
- **8**: 70-79% Tier 1-2 cards, very little filler, strong focus
- **7**: 60-69% Tier 1-2 cards, some filler, good focus
- **6**: 50-59% Tier 1-2 cards, moderate filler, decent focus
- **5**: 40-49% Tier 1-2 cards, significant filler, loose focus
- **4**: 30-39% Tier 1-2 cards, many fillers, unfocused
- **3**: 20-29% Tier 1-2 cards, mostly filler, very unfocused
- **2**: 10-19% Tier 1-2 cards, almost all filler
- **1**: <10% Tier 1-2 cards, no coherent strategy

### Section 2.5: Threat Density and Win Conditions

#### Identify Win Condition Cards

List all cards that directly contribute to winning the game:

**Primary Combos** (infinite or game-ending combinations):
- **2-card combos**: Basalt Monolith + Rings of Brighthearth, Demonic Consultation + Thassa's Oracle, Isochron Scepter + Dramatic Reversal
- **3-card combos**: Gifts Ungiven + Unburial Rites + Iona/Elesh Norn, KCI loops, etc.
- **4+ card combos**: Elaborate Rube Goldberg machines, Gitrog loops

**Compact Combos (2-3 cards)**: More powerful because they're easier to assemble and protect

**Elaborate Combos (4+ cards)**: Less powerful because they're fragile and difficult to assemble

**Combat Win Conditions**:
- **Go-wide**: Many small creatures (tokens, aristocrats)
- **Go-tall**: Single large threat (Voltron)
- **Go-explosive**: Craterhoof Behemoth, Triumph of the Hordes, Akroma's Memorial

**Alternate Win Conditions**:
- Thassa's Oracle, Laboratory Maniac
- Approach of the Second Sun
- Helix Pinnacle
- Felidar Sovereign, Test of Endurance
- Mechanized Production
- Mill (Phenax, Bruvac)

**Value Grind**:
- Out-resource opponents over time until they have no answers
- Planeswalker ultimates
- Repeatable removal + card advantage

#### Redundancy Assessment

**For each win condition, count supporting cards**:
- How many tutors can find the pieces?
- How many redundant effects exist (functional reprints)?
- Can the deck win if the primary combo piece is exiled?

**Examples**:
- **High redundancy**: Consultation/Pact + Oracle/Lab Man + 6 tutors = can find combo consistently
- **Moderate redundancy**: 1 primary combo + 1 backup combo + 2 tutors
- **Low redundancy**: 1 combo with no tutors or backups

#### Threat Deployment Speed

**What turn can threats first appear?**
- Turn 2-3: Fast mana into early threats (high power)
- Turn 4-5: On-curve threats (mid power)
- Turn 6-7: Mid-game threats (mid-low power)
- Turn 8+: Late game threats (low power)

**How quickly can threats end the game once deployed?**
- Immediately (infinite combo): Highest power
- 1-2 turn clock (Craterhoof, Voltron): High power
- 3-4 turn clock (value grind): Moderate power
- 5+ turn clock (slow beatdown): Low power

#### Evaluation Questions

1. How many distinct win conditions does the deck have?
2. How many cards support each win condition (tutors, redundancy)?
3. Can the deck win if its primary strategy is disrupted?
4. How quickly can threats end the game once deployed?
5. Are the win conditions compact (2-card) or elaborate (4+ card)?

#### Section 2.5 Scoring

**Threat Density Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: 5+ compact (2-card) win conditions, extreme redundancy, tutors for everything
- **9**: 4 compact win conditions, high redundancy, many tutors
- **8**: 3 compact win conditions, good redundancy, several tutors
- **7**: 2-3 win conditions (mix of compact and 3-card), moderate redundancy, some tutors
- **6**: 2 win conditions, some redundancy, 1-2 tutors
- **5**: 2 win conditions, limited redundancy, few tutors
- **4**: 1 primary + 1 weak backup, minimal redundancy
- **3**: 1 win condition with slight redundancy, no tutors
- **2**: 1 linear win condition, no backup
- **1**: Unclear or extremely slow win condition

---

### Section 2.6: Resilience and Protection

#### Protection Effects

**How does the deck protect its game plan from disruption?**

**Counterspells** (protecting your own plays):
- Free counters: Force of Will, Pact of Negation, Fierce Guardianship
- Efficient counters: Counterspell, Swan Song, Dispel, Flusterstorm
- Count: ___

**Hexproof/Indestructible** (permanent protection):
- Lightning Greaves, Swiftfoot Boots
- Diplomatic Immunity, Asceticism
- Heroic Intervention, Teferi's Protection
- Count: ___

**Recursion** (graveyard retrieval):
- Creature recursion: Eternal Witness, Regrowth, Noxious Revival, Animate Dead, Reanimate
- Artifact/Enchantment recursion: Replenish, Open the Vaults, Daretti
- Spell recursion: Snapcaster Mage, Underworld Breach, Mizzix's Mastery
- Count: ___

**Redundancy** (multiple copies of effects):
- Functional reprints ensure you can rebuild even if key pieces are removed
- Example: Multiple mana dorks, multiple tutors, multiple draw engines

#### Recovery Mechanisms

**Can the deck rebuild after board wipes?**
- Does it have card draw to refill hand?
- Does it have graveyard recursion to recover lost permanents?
- Are its threats resilient (indestructible, hexproof)?
- Can it combo off from an empty board?

**Can the deck pivot if the primary strategy is disrupted?**
- Are there alternative win conditions?
- Can it shift from combo to value grind if needed?
- Does it have enough interaction to slow opponents while rebuilding?

#### Evaluation Questions

1. How well can the deck protect its key pieces?
2. Can it recover from board wipes, mass discard, or graveyard hate?
3. Does it have backup plans if the primary strategy is shut down?
4. How resilient is it to targeted disruption (removal, counters)?

#### Section 2.6 Scoring

**Resilience Score (1-10)**:

- **10**: Near-impossible to disrupt, instant protection, perfect recursion, multiple backup plans
- **9**: Extensive protection (free counters, hexproof), excellent recursion, strong backups
- **8**: Strong protection suite, good recursion, can rebuild quickly
- **7**: Solid protection, some recursion, can recover with time
- **6**: Moderate protection, limited recursion, slow recovery
- **5**: Basic protection, minimal recursion, struggles after disruption
- **4**: Minimal protection, no recursion, vulnerable
- **3**: Very little protection, collapses to single board wipe
- **2**: Almost no protection, extremely fragile
- **1**: No protection or resilience, folds to any interaction

## Phase 3: Overall Deck Evaluation - Holistic Power Level Assessment

### Purpose

Synthesize findings from Phases 1 and 2 to assign a final **1-10 power level rating**.

### Section 3.1: Quantitative Metrics Scoring

Use the scores from Phase 2 to complete this weighted scorecard:

| Metric Category | Weight | Score (1-10) | Weighted Score |
|----------------|--------|--------------|----------------|
| **Speed** (from Phase 1 win turn analysis) | 20% | | |
| **Mana Base Quality** (Section 2.1) | 15% | | |
| **Fast Mana Density** (Section 2.1) | 10% | | |
| **Card Draw/Advantage** (Section 2.2) | 15% | | |
| **Interaction Quality** (Section 2.3) | 15% | | |
| **Synergy Density** (Section 2.4) | 10% | | |
| **Threat Density** (Section 2.5) | 10% | | |
| **Resilience** (Section 2.6) | 5% | | |
| **TOTAL** | **100%** | | **___/10** |

#### Speed Score (1-10)

Based on average win turn from Phase 1:

- **10**: Turn 2-3 win capability
- **9**: Turn 4 win capability
- **8**: Turn 5-6 win capability
- **7**: Turn 7-8 win capability
- **6**: Turn 9-10 win capability
- **5**: Turn 11-12 win capability
- **4**: Turn 13-14 win capability
- **3**: Turn 15-16 win capability
- **2**: Turn 17-18 win capability
- **1**: Turn 19+ win capability

**Calculation Example**:
- Total weighted score = (Speed × 0.20) + (Mana Base × 0.15) + (Fast Mana × 0.10) + (Card Draw × 0.15) + (Interaction × 0.15) + (Synergy × 0.10) + (Threats × 0.10) + (Resilience × 0.05)
- Example: (8 × 0.20) + (7 × 0.15) + (6 × 0.10) + (8 × 0.15) + (7 × 0.15) + (8 × 0.10) + (7 × 0.10) + (6 × 0.05) = 1.6 + 1.05 + 0.6 + 1.2 + 1.05 + 0.8 + 0.7 + 0.3 = **7.3/10**

This weighted score is your **base quantitative rating**.

---

### Section 3.2: Qualitative Assessment

Apply subjective judgment to contextualize the quantitative score:

#### Archetype Optimization

**How optimized is this deck for its archetype?**
- Is it running the best cards available for the strategy?
- Are there obvious upgrades being ignored (budget constraints vs. intentional choices)?
- Is the strategy itself viable at higher power levels, or is it inherently casual?

**Examples**:
- **Well-optimized**: Kinnan running all the best artifact mana and compact combos → no adjustment
- **Poorly optimized**: Kinnan running subpar ramp and 4-card combos → -1 adjustment
- **Inherently weak archetype**: Chair tribal optimized but still low power → note but don't penalize

#### Meta Positioning

**How would this deck perform in different metas?**
- Against other decks at its calculated power level?
- Against decks one power level higher/lower?
- In competitive vs. casual environments?

**Considerations**:
- Combo decks may pubstomp casual tables but struggle against interaction-heavy metas
- Stax decks may rate lower on metrics but perform above their number
- Political/group hug decks don't fit standard scaling

#### Pilot Complexity

**How difficult is this deck to pilot optimally?**
- **Linear/Straightforward (1-3)**: Play creatures, turn sideways; simple combo with clear sequencing
- **Moderate Complexity (4-7)**: Multiple decision trees, stack interactions, resource management
- **Highly Complex (8-10)**: Requires mastery, deep knowledge of interactions, tournament-level play

**Note**: Complexity ≠ power. A simple deck can be high power (fast combo), and a complex deck can be low power (elaborate jank).

#### Budget Considerations

**Are power level limitations due to budget constraints?**
- Is the deck missing expensive staples (ABUR duals, Mana Crypt, etc.)?
- If those cards were added, would the power level jump significantly?
- Is the deck intentionally budget, or is it budget by necessity?

**Important**: Budget should be **noted** but not automatically lower the rating. A well-built budget deck at power level 7 is still a 7, not a 5. Use budget notation (see Appendix).

---

### Section 3.3: Power Level Tier Comparison

Compare your quantitative score and qualitative assessment against these comprehensive tier definitions:

#### Power Level 1-2: Preconstructed/Beginner

**Description**: Unmodified precons or first-time deck builds. Learning the format.

**Characteristics**:
- No clear strategy or very basic linear plan (e.g., "play elves")
- Minimal synergy between cards (goodstuff pile)
- High CMC curve (average 4+)
- Few to no format staple cards
- **Removal**: 0-2 pieces, mostly expensive sorcery speed
- **Ramp**: Sol Ring + maybe 1-2 others, 4+ CMC average
- **Card Draw**: 0-2 sources, conditional or expensive
- **Lands**: 38-40+, all basics and tap lands
- **Tutors**: 0
- **Win Turn**: 18+, often struggles to close games
- **Synergy**: <20% Tier 1-2 cards

**Example Decks**: Unmodified Wizards precons, first deck ever built

---

#### Power Level 3-4: Casual/Upgraded Precon

**Description**: Modified precons or themed casual builds. Fun first, winning second.

**Characteristics**:
- Clear theme but loose/unfocused strategy (e.g., "dragon tribal" but no clear game plan)
- Some synergy, many filler cards (40-60% synergy)
- High CMC curve (average 3.5-4)
- Few format staples, mostly budget cards
- **Removal**: 3-5 pieces, mix of spot and board wipes, mostly sorcery speed
- **Ramp**: Sol Ring + 3-5 pieces, average CMC 3+
- **Card Draw**: 3-5 sources, often conditional
- **Lands**: 38-39, mix of tap lands and basics, minimal fixing
- **Tutors**: 0-1
- **Win Turn**: 13-17
- **Synergy**: 20-39% Tier 1-2 cards

**Example Decks**: Precon + $50-100 upgrades, tribal decks with no clear closer, theme decks (dice rolling, chairs)

---

#### Power Level 5-6: Focused/Optimized Casual

**Description**: Well-built decks with clear strategy and good card quality. The sweet spot for many playgroups.

**Characteristics**:
- Clear, focused strategy with defined game plan (knows how it wins)
- Good synergy density (40-60% Tier 1-2 cards)
- Moderate CMC curve (average 3-3.5)
- Several format staples, mix of optimized and budget cards
- **Removal**: 7-10 pieces, mix of spot/board wipes, some instant speed
- **Ramp**: Sol Ring + 6-8 pieces, 2-4 fast mana total, average CMC 2.5-3
- **Card Draw**: 6-9 sources, some engines, 1-2 tutors
- **Lands**: 36-37, decent mana base (40-50% untapped), basic fixing
- **Tutors**: 1-3
- **Win Turn**: 9-12
- **Synergy**: 40-59% Tier 1-2 cards
- 2 viable win conditions

**Example Decks**: Optimized tribal (Edgar Markov vampires), focused combo-lite (Riku value), upgraded precons with significant investment

---

#### Power Level 7-8: High Power/Optimized

**Description**: Highly tuned decks with efficient strategies and premium cards. Playing to win while maintaining some variety.

**Characteristics**:
- Streamlined, efficient strategy with clear lines to victory
- High synergy density (60-80% Tier 1-2 cards)
- Low CMC curve (average 2.5-3)
- Many format staples and archetype staples
- **Removal**: 10-12 pieces, mostly instant speed, very efficient (1-3 CMC)
- **Ramp**: 4-6 fast mana pieces, average CMC 2-2.5
- **Card Draw**: 10-12 sources, excellent engines, 3-5 tutors
- **Lands**: 34-36, strong mana base (60-70% untapped), good fixing, some fetches/duals
- **Tutors**: 4-6
- **Win Turn**: 6-8 (can threaten wins earlier but often held by interaction)
- **Synergy**: 60-79% Tier 1-2 cards
- 3+ win conditions with redundancy
- Can win through moderate disruption
- May have some budget constraints preventing cEDH status

**Example Decks**: Tuned Gitrog Monster (without crypt/cradle), optimized Yuriko, Kinnan without all the fast mana, Korvold with good but not perfect mana base

---

#### Power Level 9-10: Competitive EDH (cEDH)

**Description**: Maximum power decks built to win as fast as possible. No budget constraints, no "fun" inclusions, pure optimization.

**Characteristics**:
- Hyper-efficient strategy, often combo-focused (Turbo Naus, Consultation, etc.)
- Extreme synergy density (80%+ Tier 1-2 cards)
- Very low CMC curve (average 2-2.5 or lower)
- Only format staples and best-in-slot cards
- **Removal**: 12-15+ pieces, free spells (Force of Will, etc.), stack interaction prioritized, perfect efficiency
- **Ramp**: 6-8+ fast mana pieces (Crypt, Mox Diamond, Chrome Mox, etc.)
- **Card Draw**: 12-15+ sources, maximum selection, 6+ tutors, wheels for speed
- **Lands**: 30-33, optimized mana base (fetches/ABUR duals), perfect fixing
- **Tutors**: 6-10+
- **Win Turn**: 2-5 consistently (can threaten turn 1-2 in perfect scenarios)
- **Synergy**: 80%+ Tier 1-2 cards
- Multiple compact (2-card) win conditions with extreme redundancy
- Highly resilient, can win through heavy disruption
- No budget considerations, no "pet cards," no sub-optimal choices

**Power 9 vs. Power 10**:
- **Power 9**: Fringe cEDH—can compete at cEDH tables but not top tier (Kinnan, Yisan, Selvala)
- **Power 10**: Top tier cEDH—tournament-winning decks (Turbo Naus, PITA Oracle, Blue Farm, Sisay Paradox)

**Example Decks**: Godo Helm, The Gitrog Monster (fully optimized), Turbo Ad Nauseam, Thrasios/Tymna, K'rrik Storm

### Section 3.4: Final Rating Determination

Follow these steps to arrive at your final 1-10 rating:

#### Step 1: Start with Quantitative Score

Your base rating is the weighted score from Section 3.1.
- Example: **7.3/10**

#### Step 2: Apply Qualitative Adjustments

Based on Section 3.2 assessments:

**Archetype Optimization**:
- Well-optimized for archetype: No adjustment
- Poorly optimized (missing obvious staples): -0.5 to -1.0
- Inherently weak archetype that's well-built: Note in summary, no adjustment

**Meta Considerations**:
- Deck punches above its weight (stax, disruption-heavy): +0.5
- Deck underperforms its metrics (clunky, inconsistent): -0.5

**Example**: 7.3 base, well-optimized, no meta adjustment = **7.3**

#### Step 3: Compare to Tier Definitions

Does your adjusted score align with the tier characteristics from Section 3.3?
- 7.3 should fall into Power 7-8 range: "High Power/Optimized"
- Check if the deck's characteristics match:
  - Win turn 6-8? ✓
  - 10-12 interaction pieces? ✓
  - 4-6 fast mana? ✓
  - 60-79% Tier 1-2 synergy? ✓

If there's a mismatch between the quantitative score and tier characteristics, **favor the tier definition** (context matters more than raw numbers).

#### Step 4: Consider Multiplayer Dynamics

Does the deck's power level shift in multiplayer contexts?
- **Political decks**: May perform better than metrics suggest (threat assessment is lower)
- **Aggressive decks**: May perform worse (3 opponents at 40 life each is difficult)
- **Stax decks**: May perform better (lock down 3 opponents simultaneously)
- **Group hug/Chaos**: Don't fit standard scaling (note in summary)

#### Step 5: Sanity Check

Ask these questions:
1. Would this deck be competitive at a table of similar-rated decks?
2. Would it pubstomp lower-rated decks or get steamrolled by higher-rated ones?
3. Does the rating match the deck's actual expected performance?

If any answer is "no," revisit your assessment.

#### Step 6: Assign Final Rating

**Final Power Level**: ___/10

You may use half-points (e.g., 6.5, 7.5) for edge cases that fall between tiers.

**Rounding guidance**:
- 7.3 → Round to **7** or **7.5** depending on whether it leans toward or away from the next tier
- If the deck is a strong 7 that's approaching 8 territory, use **7.5**
- If it's a solid 7 with no signs of reaching 8, use **7**

## Evaluation Best Practices

### Phase Sequencing

**Always complete phases in order (1→2→3)**:
- Phase 1 understanding informs Phase 2 card evaluation (you need context to assess synergy)
- Phase 2 details feed into Phase 3 scoring (quantitative metrics build the base rating)

Don't skip phases or work backward—the structure is designed to build logically.

### Documentation

**Take detailed notes throughout**:
- Record specific card examples for each category
- Note standout cards (positive: "Mana Crypt pushes this to high power" or negative: "Colossal Dreadmaw is pure filler")
- Track combo pieces and synergies
- Document reasoning for scores

Good documentation helps you:
- Justify ratings to deck owners
- Calibrate against other evaluators
- Revisit evaluations later

### Objectivity Calibration

**Use the quantitative metrics as the foundation**:
- Numbers reduce bias and provide consistency
- Start with objective measurements before applying judgment
- When in doubt, compare to known reference decks (see [REFERENCE_DECKS.md](REFERENCE_DECKS.md))

**Apply qualitative judgment for edge cases**:
- Metrics can't capture everything (politics, meta, complexity)
- Use subjective assessment to contextualize numbers
- Document your reasoning for adjustments

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

**Don't let a single powerful card inflate the rating**:
- One Mana Crypt in an otherwise casual deck doesn't make it high power
- Evaluate the deck holistically, not based on spikes
- A power 4 deck with one power 9 card is still a power 4-5 deck

**Don't penalize budget versions of good decks**:
- Rate the strategy and execution, not the price tag
- A well-built budget deck at power 7 is still a 7
- Use budget notation ($ to $$$$) to communicate financial investment separately

**Don't confuse complexity with power**:
- A 10-card Rube Goldberg combo is not high power—it's fragile and inconsistent
- Simple, efficient combos (2-card) are higher power than elaborate jank
- Complexity affects pilot skill, not deck strength

**Don't ignore the social contract**:
- Stax at power 6 *feels* different than combo at power 6
- Note in the summary if the deck will draw disproportionate aggro
- Some archetypes (chaos, group hug) don't fit standard scaling—acknowledge this

**Don't rate based on ideal scenarios**:
- Goldfish testing assumes average draws, not perfect hands
- Account for disruption and interaction in real games
- A deck that *can* win turn 3 with a god hand but *averages* turn 8 is a power 7-8, not a 9

### Reference Deck Anchors

To calibrate your evaluations, use well-known decks as reference points:

- **Power 2**: Unmodified Wizards Preconstructed Deck (any year)
- **Power 4**: "Upgraded Precon" - Precon + $50-100 of targeted improvements
- **Power 6**: "Optimized Casual" - Focused tribal deck (e.g., Edgar Markov vampires with good mana base and staples)
- **Power 7**: "High Power" - Tuned combo-control deck missing expensive fast mana (e.g., Kinnan without Crypt/Cradle)
- **Power 8**: "High Power+" - Nearly optimized strategy with minor budget constraints (e.g., Gitrog Monster with good but not perfect mana base)
- **Power 9**: "Fringe cEDH" - Kinnan, Yisan, Selvala, Najeela (can win turn 3-4 with protection, compete at cEDH tables)
- **Power 10**: "Top Tier cEDH" - Turbo Naus, PITA Oracle, Blue Farm, Godo Helm (turn 2-3 wins through interaction, tournament-viable)

See [REFERENCE_DECKS.md](REFERENCE_DECKS.md) for detailed examples.

## Appendix: Advanced Considerations

### Multiplayer vs. 1v1 Adjustments

**Default evaluation assumes 4-player multiplayer**. Adjust for other contexts:

**Multiplayer-Focused Decks** (4 players):
- May rate lower on speed but higher on politics/resilience
- Examples: Group hug, political control, pillowfort
- Adjustment: Note in summary that these excel in multiplayer specifically

**1v1-Focused Decks** (Duel Commander):
- Rate higher on interaction and speed, lower on AoE effects
- Fast aggressive strategies are more viable (only 1 opponent at 40 life)
- Adjustment: If evaluating for 1v1, increase value of spot removal and decrease value of board wipes

---

### Archetype-Specific Adjustments

#### Stax/Control

**Don't penalize for low threat density**: Disruption IS the strategy.
- Emphasize resilience and interaction quality instead
- Rate based on how effectively it can lock the table
- Note: Stax decks often draw more hate than their power level suggests

**Evaluation focus**:
- How many stax pieces? (Winter Orb, Stasis, Trinisphere, etc.)
- How quickly can it establish locks?
- Can it protect its stax pieces?
- Can it win once the lock is in place?

#### Group Hug

**These decks don't fit traditional power scaling**.
- They accelerate everyone's game plans, including opponents'
- May enable higher-power decks to win faster
- Rate based on whether they "break" the balance

**Evaluation approach**:
- If it gives symmetrical advantage (Howling Mine): Lower power
- If it breaks parity (Minds Aglow + Psychosis Crawler): Higher power
- Note in summary that this deck changes game dynamics unpredictably

#### Chaos

**Similarly difficult to rate traditionally**.
- Randomizes game state, disrupts normal gameplay
- May slow games down or speed them up unpredictably

**Evaluation approach**:
- Rate based on impact on game speed and determinism
- Highly chaotic decks may be "un-ratable" (note this in summary)

#### Combo

**Emphasize speed, protection, and tutors**.
- Compact combos (2-card) rate much higher than elaborate (4+ card)
- Redundancy is critical—how many paths to the combo?
- Can it protect the combo with counterspells/hexproof?

**Evaluation focus**:
- Average turn to assemble combo
- Number of tutors to find pieces
- Interaction to protect the win
- Backup win conditions if combo is disrupted

#### Aggro/Voltron

**These struggle in multiplayer** (3 opponents at 40 life each = 120 total damage needed).
- Often rate 1-2 tiers below their goldfish speed
- Exception: Extra combat steps, Voltron with one-shot potential (21 commander damage)

**Evaluation adjustments**:
- If the deck can one-shot players (Voltron, infect): Rate based on speed to lethal
- If it needs to grind through all opponents (go-wide aggro): Rate 1-2 tiers lower than goldfish suggests
- Note in summary: "This deck's power level varies significantly based on table size"

### Budget Notation System

Add a budget indicator to ratings for clarity:

**$$$$** (Optimized): No budget constraints, all best-in-slot cards (Mana Crypt, ABUR duals, etc.)

**$$$** (High Budget): Most staples present, missing some expensive pieces ($1000-3000 deck value)

**$$** (Medium Budget): Good card quality, budget alternatives in play ($300-1000 deck value)

**$** (Budget): Primarily budget cards, few expensive staples (<$300 deck value)

**Example**: "Power Level 7 ($$)" indicates a well-built deck at medium budget hitting power 7.

**Purpose**: Distinguishes between "Power 7 with Mana Crypt" and "Power 7 with budget mana base." Both are power 7, but one has significant room for upgrades.

---

### Meta-Dependent Considerations

Note when a deck's rating might shift based on local meta:

**Graveyard-heavy meta**: Graveyard hate becomes more valuable
- Example: Rest in Peace in a graveyard-heavy meta punches above its usual power

**Combo-heavy meta**: Interaction density matters more
- Stack interaction and disruption are premium
- Slow value decks may underperform their metrics

**Battlecruiser meta**: Board wipes and creature removal emphasized
- Single-target removal is less valuable
- Mass removal is premium

**Fast meta (cEDH or high power)**: Speed and protection paramount
- Turn 6+ win conditions may be too slow
- Free interaction (Force of Will) is essential

**Adjustment**: Note in summary if the deck is particularly well- or poorly-positioned for specific metas.

---

### Continuous Calibration

**Rubric maintenance**:
- As new sets release, power creep may shift tier definitions
- Regularly revisit reference decks to ensure calibration
- Update tier characteristics if the format evolves significantly

**Evaluator calibration**:
- Compare your ratings to other evaluators regularly
- Discuss discrepancies to align understanding
- Use reference decks to establish common baselines

**Feedback loop**:
- Track actual game performance vs. ratings
- If decks consistently over- or under-perform their ratings, revisit the evaluation
- Adjust tier definitions or scoring weights if systematic issues emerge
