# MTG Commander Deck Evaluation Rubric

A comprehensive framework for evaluating Magic: The Gathering Commander/EDH decks on a **1-10 power level scale**.

## Overview

This rubric provides structured evaluation criteria for assessing Commander deck power levels, enabling accurate matchmaking and balanced gameplay. It combines objective metrics with necessary subjective judgment to produce consistent, actionable ratings.

### Why Use This Rubric?

- **Structured approach**: Three-phase evaluation ensures thorough, consistent assessment
- **Balances objectivity**: Weighted quantitative scorecard reduces bias
- **Commander-specific**: Accounts for singleton format, multiplayer dynamics, and social contract
- **Actionable ratings**: Produces 1-10 power levels for matchmaking
- **Comprehensive**: Covers all archetypes, budget levels, and edge cases

### Who Is This For?

- **Playgroup organizers** establishing deck guidelines
- **Store owners** running Commander events
- **Deck builders** wanting honest assessment of their creations
- **Players** seeking appropriately-matched games
- **Content creators** reviewing and rating Commander decks

## Three-Phase Evaluation Process

### Phase 1: Initial Analysis (10-15 minutes)

**Goal**: Understand the deck's strategy before evaluating individual cards

**Tasks**:
1. Identify deck archetype and win conditions
2. Assess speed and consistency (goldfish testing)
3. Document ideal game plan

**Output**: 3-5 sentence strategy summary

### Phase 2: Card-Level Evaluation (30-45 minutes)

**Goal**: Assess how well each card contributes to the deck's strategy

**Six Categories**:
1. Mana Base Evaluation (lands + ramp)
2. Card Draw and Card Advantage
3. Interaction and Removal
4. Synergy and Card Quality
5. Threat Density and Win Conditions
6. Resilience and Protection

**Output**: Quantitative scores (1-10) for each category

### Phase 3: Overall Deck Evaluation (10-15 minutes)

**Goal**: Synthesize findings into a final 1-10 power level rating

**Tasks**:
1. Calculate weighted quantitative scorecard (base rating)
2. Apply qualitative adjustments (archetype optimization, meta, complexity, budget)
3. Compare to tier definitions
4. Sanity check and finalize rating

**Output**: Final 1-10 power level with comprehensive summary

## Key Evaluation Principles

### Objectivity

- **Start with numbers**: Quantitative scorecard provides foundation
- **Use reference decks**: Compare to known examples for calibration
- **Document reasoning**: Explain scores and adjustments

### Context Matters

- **Archetype differences**: Stax, Group Hug, Chaos don't fit standard scaling
- **Multiplayer dynamics**: Voltron and Aggro struggle; Stax and Politics excel
- **Meta positioning**: Note when decks are particularly well- or poorly-positioned

### Favor Tier Definitions

- **If quantitative score conflicts with tier characteristics**, favor the tier definition
- Numbers are a guide, not absolute truth
- Context and actual performance matter more than raw metrics

### Budget Notation

- **Don't penalize budget decks**: Rate the strategy, not the price tag
- **Use budget indicators**: $ to $$$$ communicates financial investment separately
- **Note upgrade paths**: "Power 6 ($$)—would be Power 7-8 with Mana Crypt and ABUR duals"

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### During Evaluation

1. **Don't let one powerful card inflate the rating**: Mana Crypt in a Power 4 deck doesn't make it Power 6
2. **Don't confuse complexity with power**: Elaborate combos ≠ high power
3. **Don't rate based on best-case scenarios**: Use average win turn, not nut draws
4. **Don't ignore coverage**: 12 pieces of narrow interaction < 8 pieces of broad interaction
5. **Don't penalize budget alternatives**: A well-built budget deck can still be high power

### After Evaluation

1. **Don't be defensive**: If someone disagrees, discuss objectively
2. **Don't skip the sanity check**: "Would this deck compete at a table of similar-rated decks?"
3. **Don't forget to document**: Note special considerations in your summary
4. **Don't rate in a vacuum**: Compare to reference decks before finalizing

## Matchmaking Guidelines

### Power Level Ranges

- **±1 power level** produces balanced games
  - Power 6 deck → Pair with Power 5-7 decks
  - Power 8 deck → Pair with Power 7-9 decks

- **±2 or more** creates imbalances (pubstomps or frustration)

### Special Considerations

- **cEDH (Power 9-10)**: Only pair with other cEDH decks
- **Stax**: May draw more hate than power level suggests
- **Group Hug**: Accelerates all players; may enable higher-power wins
- **Combo vs. Combat**: Different pacing but can be balanced at same power level

## Example Evaluation Summary

**Deck Name**: Edgar Markov - Vampire Tribal
**Commander**: Edgar Markov
**Archetype**: Vampire Tribal Aggro

**Final Power Level**: **6/10**
**Tier**: Focused/Optimized Casual
**Budget Indicator**: $$

**Quantitative Base Score**: 6.0/10

**Phase 1 Summary**: Focused vampire tribal that generates wide board presence through Edgar's eminence, winning through combat damage with anthems and tribal synergies. Can pivot to aristocrats strategies with Blood Artist effects. Average win turn is 10-11.

**Phase 2 Scores**:
- Speed: 6/10 (wins turn 10-11)
- Mana Base: 6/10 (36 lands, 45% Tier 1-2)
- Fast Mana: 5/10 (4 pieces)
- Card Draw: 7/10 (9 sources)
- Interaction: 6/10 (8 pieces)
- Synergy: 6/10 (55% Tier 1-2)
- Threats: 6/10 (2 win conditions, 2 tutors)
- Resilience: 5/10 (some protection, limited recursion)

**Key Strengths**:
1. Strong tribal synergies create explosive mid-game
2. Edgar's eminence provides value without casting
3. Multiple paths to victory (combat, aristocrats)

**Key Weaknesses**:
1. Vulnerable to board wipes
2. Struggles against faster combo decks
3. Mana base could be optimized

**Recommended Matchmaking**: Power 5-7 decks
